Prototo Desktop puts the whole product in one window. Your coding agent on the left (you pick Claude Code or Codex during setup). A live iPhone on the right. Record, Preview, and Publish buttons up top.

[Download Prototo for Mac](https://prototo.app/download), open it, and create a project. That is the setup.

```
Prototo Desktop (one window)
 ├── Agent terminal     ← the design tool (left pane)
 ├── live iPhone        ← the canvas (right pane)
 └── Record · Preview · Publish   ← record a clip, phone preview, share a link
```

Describe a screen in the terminal. Watch it appear on the iPhone beside it. Want to change one thing on it? Turn on the accessibility overlay in the preview's dock, tap the element, and it lands in your prompt ready for the change ([point and edit](/prompting/point-and-edit)). Click **Preview** to scan a QR and hold the prototype on your real iPhone while you build, no publish needed ([how it works](/getting-started/preview-on-iphone)). When it looks right, click **Publish** for a link anyone can run natively on their iPhone with the free Prototo app, or **Record** to send a clip to Studio.

On a [Team plan](/sharing/plans), **Publish** asks where a prototype goes: **Team**, so everyone on your team sees it, or **Just me**. The **Team** button opens your team on prototo.app, where you can remix anything the team has published ([how remix works](/sharing/remix)). Everything else in the app works the same whether you're signed in or not.

The app keeps everything else out of sight: it starts Prototo for you, opens the Simulator hidden, and reconnects your last project when you come back.

The paradigm stays clean: one prompt surface, one output surface. You never context-switch between design tools.

Prefer raw Terminal windows? The old flow still works: see [the terminal flow](/getting-started/terminal).
